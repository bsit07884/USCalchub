How This Calculation Works

Federal Income Tax

Calculated using 2026 progressive IRS brackets (10% to 37%). Single standard deduction is set to $14,600. Source: IRS Pub 15-T.

FICA (SS & Medicare)

Social Security (6.2% on wages up to $168,600) + Medicare (1.45% on all earnings). High-earner 0.9% additional surtax applies over $200k.

State Income Tax

9 states charge 0% (TX, FL, NV, WY, WA, TN, SD, AK, NH). California tops out at 13.3%, while New York levies up to 10.9%.

Private by Design

All calculations run entirely in your local web browser. No financial data is ever collected, stored, tracked, or sent to any server.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which state gives you the highest take-home pay on a $75,000 salary?
The states with zero personal earned income tax (Texas, Florida, Nevada, Washington, Wyoming, Tennessee, South Dakota, Alaska, and New Hampshire) deliver the absolute maximum take-home pay of approximately $61,593 per year ($5,133/month) for a single filer claiming standard deductions.
How much less do I take home in California compared to Texas?
In California, a single earner on a $75,000 gross salary pays approximately $3,463 in state income tax plus State Disability Insurance (SDI). As a result, you take home roughly $58,130 in California versus $61,593 in Texas — an immediate difference of $3,463 net cash in pocket per year.
Are these figures accurate for the 2026 tax year?
Yes. These figures use official IRS Publication 15-T standard withholding tax brackets, the updated Social Security wage base limit, the single standard deduction baseline, and enacted 2026 state tax statutes.
