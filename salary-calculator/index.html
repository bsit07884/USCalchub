How is Your Take-Home Pay Calculated?

Your gross salary is just the starting point. Before your paycheck hits your bank account, several mandatory deductions are taken out:

  • Federal Income Tax: A progressive tax system where your income is divided into brackets (10%, 12%, 22%, etc.) and taxed accordingly.
  • FICA Taxes: This consists of a 6.2% deduction for Social Security and a 1.45% deduction for Medicare, totaling 7.65% for most W-2 employees.
  • State Income Tax: Depending on your location, this can range from 0% in states like Florida and Nevada, up to over 13% in California for high earners.
  • Local Taxes: Some cities and municipalities (like New York City) impose their own additional taxes.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. How much is $60,000 a year after taxes?

In 2026, a single filer earning $60,000 will pay approximately $5,000 in federal tax and $4,590 in FICA. After state taxes, your actual take-home pay for $60k usually ranges between $45,800 in high-tax states to $50,400 in no-tax states.

2. How much is $75,000 a year after taxes?

For a $75,000 salary, a single filer's net pay will generally fall between $55,800 and $61,960 annually. Read our full $75,000 salary after tax breakdown to see exact comparisons across all 50 states.

3. Which states have no income tax in 2026?

There are currently nine US states with no state income tax: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. Living in these states significantly increases your net take-home pay.

4. How is my paycheck different in California vs Texas?

California imposes one of the highest progressive state income taxes in the nation, while Texas has 0% state income tax. On an $80,000 salary, moving from California to Texas can instantly increase your take-home pay by thousands of dollars per year without needing a raise.

5. What's the difference between gross pay and net pay?

Gross pay is your total earned salary before any taxes or deductions are taken out (e.g., $75,000). Net pay, also known as take-home pay, is the actual amount of money deposited into your bank account after federal taxes, state taxes, FICA, and benefits are deducted.

6. What are FICA taxes?

FICA stands for the Federal Insurance Contributions Act. It is a mandatory payroll tax split between employers and employees to fund Social Security and Medicare. For W-2 employees, the combined FICA rate is 7.65%.

7. How do I calculate my take-home pay?

To manually calculate your take-home pay, start with your gross income, subtract your federal standard deduction, apply your marginal tax brackets, deduct 7.65% for FICA, and finally subtract your specific state and local taxes.
