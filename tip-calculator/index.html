Understanding US Tipping Culture in 2026

Unlike many other countries, service industry workers in the United States often rely heavily on tips to make a living wage. Tipping in the US varies significantly by service type. Here is a quick reference guide for common scenarios:

  • Restaurants (Sit-down): 15% for average service, 18% for good service, and 20% or more for excellent service.
  • Food Delivery: Typically 10% to 15% of the total bill, or a flat $3 to $5 minimum depending on the distance, traffic, and weather conditions.
  • Salons and Spas: A 15% to 20% gratuity is the standard expectation for haircuts, massages, and personal care services.
  • Takeout / Pick-up: Tipping is generally optional, but leaving 5% to 10% is highly appreciated for large, complicated, or curbside orders.

How This Calculation Works

Tip Formula

Tip Amount = Bill Total × (Tip % ÷ 100). Total = Bill + Tip. Per Person = Total ÷ Number of People. Always calculate tip BEFORE splitting for accuracy.

Pre-Tax vs Post-Tax Tipping

Standard practice is to tip on the pre-tax bill amount. However, most people calculate on the total including tax for simplicity. The difference on a $100 bill is usually under $2.

Splitting Uneven Bills

When people ordered differently, calculate each person's share of the bill first, add their proportional tip (based on their subtotal), then sum for their total. This calculator uses equal split for simplicity.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. How do I split a bill fairly when everyone ordered different amounts?

When orders are uneven, the most precise method is to calculate each person's individual items, then add their proportional share of the tax and the tip. Our tip calculator provides a simple even split, but for complex situations, read our detailed guide on how to split a bill fairly.

2. Should the tip be split equally even if people ordered different amounts?

If the bill varies drastically (e.g., someone ordered an expensive steak and cocktails while another had a side salad), it is fairer to calculate the tip proportionally based on each person's subtotal. However, for relatively similar orders, an equal split is the quickest and most socially common practice.

3. What is a fair tip for bad service?

In the US, leaving no tip is considered highly offensive, as servers rely on gratuities to meet minimum wage requirements. If the service was exceptionally poor, a 10% tip sends a clear message without completely depriving the worker of their income. It is also recommended to politely express your concerns to a manager.

4. How much should I tip for delivery or takeout?

For food delivery, a standard tip is 10% to 15%, with a minimum of $3 to $5. If the weather is severe or the order is unusually large, a 20% tip is appropriate. For takeout or pickup, tipping is technically optional, but leaving 5% to 10% is customary for well-packaged orders.

5. Is gratuity calculated before or after tax?

Standard tipping etiquette dictates that you calculate the tip based on the pre-tax subtotal of your bill. However, many modern point-of-sale systems calculate their suggested percentage tips on the post-tax total, resulting in a slightly higher tip amount.

6. What is the standard tipping percentage in 2026?

The national average for standard sit-down restaurant service remains between 18% and 20% in 2026. Exceptional service often warrants 22% to 25%, while basic service typically merits around 15%.
