How This Calculation Works

Overtime Premium Formula

Premium = 0.5 × Regular Hourly Rate × Overtime Hours. This is the "extra" half-pay you receive above your regular rate for overtime hours. Only this premium portion qualifies for the 2026 exemption.

$12,500 Annual Cap

The maximum tax-free deduction is $12,500 per year. If your total overtime premium exceeds $12,500, only the first $12,500 is exempt from federal income tax. FICA taxes still apply to all overtime.

Who Qualifies

Hourly workers and non-exempt salaried employees receiving 1.5× pay under FLSA. Independent contractors and exempt salaried employees do not qualify.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. Is overtime pay taxed differently in 2026?

Yes. Under the new 2026 tax provisions, a specific portion of your overtime pay—specifically the "premium" portion (the extra 0.5x in time-and-a-half)—can be exempt from federal income taxes, allowing hourly workers to keep significantly more of their extra earnings.

2. What is the $12,500 overtime tax deduction cap?

The 2026 legislation caps the tax-exempt overtime premium at $12,500 annually. This means you can earn up to $12,500 in pure overtime premium pay without paying standard federal income taxes on that specific amount.

3. How is overtime premium calculated?

Overtime is typically paid at "time and a half" (1.5x your regular hourly rate). The "premium" is the extra 0.5x. For example, if your base rate is $20/hr, your overtime rate is $30/hr. The premium portion is the extra $10/hr, which is the part eligible for the new 2026 tax deductions.

4. Who qualifies for the overtime tax exemption?

The exemption primarily targets W-2 hourly workers who are eligible for overtime under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). It is designed to benefit blue-collar and shift workers who rely on overtime hours to supplement their base income.

5. Do salaried employees qualify for overtime tax deductions?

Generally, no. Most salaried employees are classified as "exempt" under the FLSA, meaning they do not legally receive overtime pay, and thus cannot claim the overtime tax deduction. However, salaried non-exempt workers who are legally paid overtime may still qualify.

6. How much can I save with the 2026 overtime tax exemption?

Your exact savings depend on your top marginal tax bracket. If you max out the $12,500 premium cap and fall into the 22% federal tax bracket, this exemption could save you approximately $2,750 a year in federal taxes.
