Quick Answer & IRS Thresholds

In 2026, single filers with combined income above $34,000 have up to 85% of SS benefits taxable. Below $25,000 — completely tax-free. For married filing jointly: tax-free below $32,000, and up to 85% taxable above $44,000.

How This Calculation Works

Combined Income Formula

Combined Income = AGI + Nontaxable Interest + 50% of Social Security Benefits. This IRS metric determines benefit taxability.

The 85% Maximum Rule

The IRS can tax at most 85% of your benefits. At least 15% is always 100% tax-free regardless of your income level.

State Tax Rules

42 states + D.C. charge 0% state tax on Social Security. Only 8 states tax benefits, each offering distinct income, age, or credit exemptions.

Private by Design

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Do I have to pay taxes on Social Security if it's my only income?

In most cases, no. If your only source of income is your Social Security benefit, your Combined Income will typically fall well below the IRS base amount ($25,000 for singles, $32,000 for married couples). Therefore, your benefits will remain 100% tax-free at both federal and state levels.

2. What counts as Combined Income for Social Security taxation?

The IRS uses a specific formula to calculate your Combined Income (also known as provisional income). It includes your Adjusted Gross Income (AGI), plus any nontaxable interest (such as municipal bond distributions), plus exactly one-half (50%) of your total Social Security benefits.

3. Can I reduce the tax I pay on my Social Security benefits?

Yes. Common strategies include managing withdrawals from traditional IRAs/401(k)s, utilizing Roth IRA conversions prior to retirement, taking Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs) after age 70½, and timing capital gains to stay below the $25k/$32k base thresholds.

4. Is Social Security taxed differently in each state?

Absolutely. While this calculator estimates your federal tax liability, your state tax burden depends entirely on where you live. In 2026, 42 states and Washington D.C. (including West Virginia) do not tax Social Security benefits at all.

5. What is the maximum percentage of Social Security that can be taxed?

Under federal law, the absolute maximum amount of your Social Security benefits that can be subject to federal income tax is 85%. You will never be taxed on 100% of your benefits, no matter how high your external income is.
