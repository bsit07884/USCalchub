Post-NAR Settlement Rules: How They Apply to a $750,000 Home

In the $750,000 price range, commission dollars grow large enough that traditional percentage fees face heightened competition. Under post-NAR rules requiring written buyer representation agreements, buyers are acutely aware of broker compensation, giving $750k sellers greater leverage to negotiate customized compensation structures.

Market Tier Analysis

Move-Up Suburban Markets & Dual-Transaction Leverage

At $750,000—common for move-up family homes in competitive suburban enclaves—a 6% commission reaches a significant $45,000. Because the broker's workload to market a $750,000 home is rarely double that of a $375,000 home, sellers possess substantial negotiating leverage.

A primary strategy in this bracket is dual-transaction representation: if you hire the same real estate agent to sell your existing $750,000 home and purchase your next property, agents will frequently discount their listing commission to 1.5% or 2.0% ($11,250 to $15,000) in exchange for the guaranteed purchase business.

Pairing a 2.0% listing fee with a 2.5% buyer-broker concession reduces your total commission outlay to 4.5% ($33,750)—saving you $11,250 in cash compared to an unnegotiated 6.0% standard.

Frequently Asked Questions ($750,000 Sale)

How much commission do realtors make on a $750,000 home?

At 5% to 6%, total commission ranges from $37,500 to $45,000. Under an equal split, the listing agent's office and buyer agent's office each receive $18,750 to $22,500.

What are the net proceeds from selling a $750k house?

After deducting 5% to 6% in real estate agent commissions, your net proceeds from the sale are between $705,000 and $712,500. Routine transfer taxes, title fees, and mortgage payoff will be subtracted from this remaining balance.

Can I use a 1% listing agent on a $750,000 house?

Yes. Many full-service discount brokerages offer 1.0% to 1.5% listing fees ($7,500 to $11,250). If you pair a 1.5% listing fee with a 2.5% buyer concession, your total commission is 4.0% ($30,000), saving you $15,000 compared to a 6% fee.
