How Real Estate Commissions Work in 2026

Understanding decoupled representation fees and closing costs.

⚖

Post-NAR Settlement Decoupling

Sellers are no longer required to offer unilateral commission splits to buyer agents on MLS systems. Every representation agreement is strictly between the client and their broker.

🤝

Dual-Agent Representation Rules

When one agent represents both buyer and seller (where permitted by state law), total commission is frequently reduced to 3.5%–4.5% since marketing and administrative overhead is halved.

📑

Closing Costs vs. Commission

Real estate commissions constitute the largest seller expense (typically 5-6%), while title transfer fees, escrow, recording, and transfer taxes typically total an additional 1% to 2%.

🔒

100% Private & Browser-Calculated

All calculations run locally on your device. We do not track property addresses, sale values, or contact information. No lead capture forms or unsolicited agent phone calls.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Who pays the buyer's agent commission under 2026 rules?▼

Traditionally, the seller paid the entirety of the 5% to 6% commission, split between both brokerages. Under current rules, buyers sign direct representation agreements with their broker. However, sellers may still offer seller concessions at closing to cover buyer agent costs to broaden the pool of eligible buyers.

2. Are real estate commissions set by law?▼

No. By federal antitrust law, commission rates are entirely negotiable between property owners and licensed brokers. There is no statutory minimum or maximum fee anywhere in the United States.

3. What additional closing costs will a seller pay beyond commission?▼

Beyond commission fees, sellers typically cover state and county transfer taxes (deed stamps), title search & owner’s title insurance policies, outstanding property prorations, HOA transfer fees, and attorney/escrow settlement charges. These typically amount to 1% to 2% of the home price.
