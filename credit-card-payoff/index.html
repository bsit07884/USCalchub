How This Calculation Works

Monthly Interest Formula

Monthly Interest = Balance × (APR ÷ 12 ÷ 100). Each month, interest is added to your balance before your payment is applied. This is why minimum payments barely reduce principal.

Minimum Payment

Calculated as 2% of current balance or $25 — whichever is greater. As your balance decreases, so does your minimum payment, extending payoff time dramatically.

Debt Avalanche Method

If you have multiple cards, pay minimums on all cards and direct every extra dollar to the highest APR card first. This minimizes total interest paid across all debts.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What is a payoff on a credit card?

A payoff on a credit card refers to paying the entire outstanding balance, including any accrued interest and fees, to bring the account balance to exactly zero. You can use our credit card payoff calculator to map out the exact timeline to reach this goal.

2. What is the correct way to pay off a credit card?

The most financially sound method is to pay your statement balance in full every single month to avoid interest charges entirely. If you are carrying a balance, consistently pay more than the minimum requirement and stop using the card for new purchases while you pay it down.

3. What happens if I only pay the minimum payment?

Paying only the minimum amount stretches your debt over years or even decades. The majority of your payment will go toward interest charges rather than the principal balance, causing you to pay significantly more money to the bank over time.

4. How much should I pay extra each month to pay off my card faster?

There is no set rule, but any extra dollar helps. Even a small additional amount—like $50 or $100 above your minimum—can drastically reduce your repayment timeline and save you hundreds in interest.

5. What is the difference between debt avalanche and debt snowball method?

The debt avalanche method focuses on paying off cards with the highest interest rates first to save the most money. The debt snowball method focuses on paying off the smallest balances first to gain psychological momentum. Both are excellent strategies for becoming debt-free.

6. How do I get the payoff amount for my credit card?

You can find your exact payoff amount by logging into your online banking portal or calling your credit card issuer. The payoff amount is usually slightly higher than your current balance because it includes interest calculated up to the exact day you plan to make the payment.

7. What is a credit card payoff loan and is it better?

A credit card payoff loan (or debt consolidation loan) is a personal loan used to pay off your credit card balances. It can be highly beneficial if it offers a significantly lower interest rate than your current cards. You can use a credit card payoff calculator to compare your current interest costs against the fees of a new loan.

8. How is the total interest calculated?

Credit card issuers calculate interest based on your Average Daily Balance and your Annual Percentage Rate (APR). The APR is divided by 365 to get your daily periodic rate, which is then multiplied by your daily balance to determine the interest charges added to your account.
