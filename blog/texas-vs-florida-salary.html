Moving South? Texas vs. Florida Take-Home Pay in 2026

Packing up and moving to a state with no income tax sounds like a financial dream. But how much extra cash will actually hit your bank account? Let’s break it down.

If you are planning to relocate in 2026, you are not alone. Thousands of Americans are leaving high-tax states like California and New York, heading straight for the Sun Belt. Texas and Florida are the top two destinations, and they share one massive financial perk: zero state income tax.

The "$75,000 Salary" Example

Let’s say you land a job making $75,000 a year. If you lived in New York, state and local taxes would take a significant bite out of every paycheck. But in Texas or Florida, your paycheck calculation becomes much simpler.

Since neither state charges income tax, your deductions are purely federal. Here is what happens to that $75k:

  • Federal Income Tax: Applies to everyone, regardless of where you live.
  • FICA Taxes: This is your Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%) contribution.
  • State Tax: $0.00 (The magic number!).

So, are Texas and Florida financially identical?

Not exactly. While your take-home pay will be identical in both states for the exact same salary, your cost of living will differ. This is the catch that most people miss.

States have to fund their roads, schools, and services somehow. Since they aren't taxing your paycheck, they tax your lifestyle:

  • Texas: Has some of the highest property taxes in the country. If you plan to buy a house, a large chunk of your tax savings will go straight into property taxes.
  • Florida: Generally has lower property taxes than Texas, but makes up for it with higher sales taxes in tourist-heavy counties and significantly higher home insurance premiums.
